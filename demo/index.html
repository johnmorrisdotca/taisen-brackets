Judo scoreboard

The scoreboard of one judo contest, drawn by judoScoreboard for the state you set: the athletes with their flags and codes, the ippon, waza-ari and yuko counters, the shido cards, the clock, golden score and the osaekomi timer, on a TV, a tablet or an upright tablet, as a screen board or as table-top flip cards. Live updating is your job: draw again with the new state. The state is in the address and nowhere else. The names are invented.

Contest card and mat queue board

Two more screens for a judo event, drawn by judoContestCard and judoMatQueue: the card shown when the athletes enter (the category and round, the white athlete over the blue one, flags and codes), and the board of upcoming contests per mat, each mat with its big number and colour and the contests in order with their numbers. Choose how many mats; the first contest of a mat is the one on now. The athletes are invented.

Road to the final of a judoka

The panel for a broadcast or a hall screen: one athlete's block (flag, name, code, coach) and the contests they have won on the way, most recent first, each with its round, the opponent and how it was won. judoWins reads the wins from the bracket of the Judo example above (the same made-up men's -66 kg category); choose any of its 16 athletes, a bronze medallist's repechage and bronze medal contest included. A bye is a quiet row.

Rankings

A ranking for any sport, drawn by rankingList from your own data: a title and a category, then the entries as a podium (the top three in gold, silver and bronze above a compact list), as one featured row above two columns, or as a plain list. Text first, with no photo to leave empty: rank, flag (a country's or a Canadian province's), name, club, points, and the movement since the last list, with newly ranked athletes marked NR, tied ranks, a champion row (C) and paging. rankingNumberOnes draws the number one of each category as a card, and rankingEntryList an event's entry list in the order of the draw with each athlete's ranking beside the name (NR where there is none), and rankingMatchup the head-to-head card of two athletes with their previous meetings and the record. The athletes and clubs are invented; the flags are only there to show a country's and a province's.

Medal table

A medal table for any sport, drawn by medalTable: ranked gold first, as the Olympic table is, or by the total, with equal rows sharing a rank (5, 5, 7), a flag or a badge for each country or team, and the three medals and the total. tallyMedals adds up a list of medals, filtered to one sport, one weight class or one Games, so that one list gives the table of an event, of a sport and of all time. The medals are invented, and the former countries (the Soviet Union, West Germany, Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia, the Unified Team) are drawn as badges with their codes, as Hata has no flags for them yet.

Broadcast boards

Boards in the manner of a results graphic on a television broadcast, for a screen or to place over a picture: a title band with an icon, a sub bar, a row for each athlete with its rank, country code, a 4:3 flag, the name, a record badge (OR, WR, NR) and one or more columns of scores or times at the sport's own precision. resultsBoard returns a standalone SVG of the width you choose and a height that follows from the rows (resultsBoardSize, shown below). Nothing of any games, federation or broadcaster is drawn. The athletes and results are invented.
